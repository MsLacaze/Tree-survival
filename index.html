🌧️ A change of plan!

The class was supposed to plant more seedlings for Trees for Survival, but the weather has other ideas. The seedlings still need you!

Your challenge: You have 100 young native trees ready to be planted. Work out what could threaten them, how to protect them and how to give them the best chance of growing into healthy trees.

How to play

  1. Work with a partner or team.
  2. Read each mission carefully.
  3. Discuss your ideas before typing them.
  4. Record your best answers in your book.
  5. Complete the knowledge checks to unlock the next mission.

🔍 Mission 1: What Can Kill a Seedling?

Before you can protect a tree, understand what can go wrong. Brainstorm as many threats as you can.

Challenge A — The brainstorm

List at least six different things that could cause a newly planted seedling to die or struggle to grow.

Think about weather, soil, animals, people, weeds and the seedling itself. Separate ideas with commas or new lines.

Challenge B — Quick-fire knowledge check

Which could compete with a young tree for water, light and nutrients?

🛠️ Mission 2: Save the Seedling!

Five young trees are in trouble. Read each situation and decide what the problem is and how you would help.

🐇 Case 1: The Hungry Rabbits

You planted 20 seedlings. Two weeks later, half have had their leaves and stems eaten.

☀️ Case 2: The Summer Heatwave

Your seedlings were planted just before three weeks without rain. The soil is dry and leaves are drooping.

🌿 Case 3: The Weed Invasion

Grass and weeds are growing rapidly around your young trees.

💨 Case 4: The Windy Hill

Your seedlings are planted on an exposed hillside. Strong winds keep bending them over.

💧 Case 5: The Waterlogged Ground

Your seedlings are sitting in constantly wet soil after days of heavy rain.

🌟 Team discussion: Choose the case you think is the biggest threat to newly planted trees. Be ready to defend your choice.

🏗️ Mission 3: Design a Tree Protection System

Your team has been asked to design a protection system for a newly planted native tree. It must help the tree survive its first year.

Your design must include:

  • Protection from browsing animals.
  • Protection from weeds competing with the tree.
  • A way to help retain soil moisture.
  • Support or shelter from harsh weather, where needed.
  • A way to help the tree establish healthy roots.

Think about practical materials such as guards, stakes, mulch and weed-free circles. Consider how to avoid harming wildlife or the environment.

Use your finger, mouse or trackpad to sketch. You can also draw a larger labelled version in your book.

🌱 Mission 4: The Tree Survival Expert Test

Final test! Answer these questions to show what you have learned about helping seedlings survive.

1. Why should weeds and grass be controlled around a newly planted seedling?

2. Why can constantly waterlogged soil damage a tree's roots?

3. What is one useful reason to put mulch around a young tree, while keeping it clear of the trunk?

4. Why is aftercare important after planting?

5. What is the best way to judge whether a tree-planting project has been successful?
